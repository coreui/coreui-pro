## How it works

A block is markup you copy into your project, not a component you import. Every block on this page is a [card](/components/card/) plus [spacing](/utilities/margin/), [color](/utilities/colors/) and [typography](/utilities/font-size/) utilities, so it ships no CSS of its own and there is no class here that the rest of the library does not already document.

That is the whole point of keeping them as blocks: a statistic card is a layout decision. Once the markup is yours, a widget can grow a second value, lose its chart or change its heading level without waiting for a prop.

The chart areas below are placeholders. Any charting library renders into them — the blocks only reserve the space and set its height.

## Stat group

Several figures in one card, divided by a border, under a header that holds the chart.

<Example code={`<div class="card">
    <div class="card-header p-0 text-bg-primary">
      <svg class="docs-placeholder-img d-block" width="100%" height="70" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" role="img" aria-label="Chart" preserveAspectRatio="none" focusable="false"><title>Chart</title><rect width="100%" height="100%" fill="rgba(255,255,255,.2)"></rect></svg>
    </div>
    <div class="card-body">
      <div class="row text-center w-100">
        <div class="col">
          <div class="fs-5 fw-semibold">89.9%</div>
          <div class="text-uppercase fg-2 small">Sessions</div>
        </div>
        <div class="col border-start">
          <div class="fs-5 fw-semibold">12.124</div>
          <div class="text-uppercase fg-2 small">Users</div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>`} />

A `.card-body` lays its children out in a column and packs them to the start, so anything meant to sit side by side inside one needs `.flex-row`, and a `.row` needs `.w-100` to fill the card. Putting `.row` on the body itself does not work: the body's own `flex-direction: column` wins and the columns stack.
